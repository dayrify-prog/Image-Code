The Idea

Wing I · The IdeaRoom 01 — Concept
01

A Personal Signature

An elegant identity shaped around personal image, color harmony and individual expression. Like a signature, it should feel drawn by hand, and belong to one person only.

Form

Delicate strokes and considered proportions. Nothing is heavier than it needs to be.

Character

An expressive monogram of I and C, editorial rather than corporate.

Tone

Refined, warm and quietly confident. The brand never raises its voice.

Feeling

A private appointment in a beautiful room: calm, attentive, unhurried.

Wing I · The IdeaRoom 02 — Brand Story
02

Why We Exist

Three documents hang in this room. Together they explain what Image Code does, where it is going, and what every client can count on.

Positioning statement

For people who want to look like the best version of themselves, Image Code is the studio that turns color analysis into a personal code: precise, wearable and entirely their own.

Tagline

The Art of Being Yourself

Descriptor

Personal Color Analysis · Image & Styling

Boilerplate

Image Code is a studio for personal color analysis, image and styling. Through a precise, considered method, it helps each client find the colors, lines and presence that are naturally theirs, and build a wardrobe and image around them.

Wing I · The IdeaRoom 04 — Audience
04

Who Walks Through the Door

Clients rarely arrive asking for color analysis. They arrive at a moment in their lives. Design and copy should speak to the moment first.

What they share

They value expertise over trends, privacy over spectacle, and results they can keep using long after the session ends.

What they fear

Being judged, being sold to, or being turned into someone they are not. The brand should remove all three.

Wing I · The IdeaRoom 05 — Voice & Tone
05

How Image Code Speaks

The voice of a trusted consultant: someone who knows a great deal and shares it gently. Hover each card to see the version we never write.

I

Knowing, never lecturing

Explain the why in one line, then move on.

II

Warm, never sugary

Kind and direct. No gushing, no exclamation marks.

III

Precise, never technical

Name things exactly, without jargon the client must decode.

IV

Personal, never presumptuous

Talk to "you". Never assume what someone should want.

We say

"These tones bring light to your face."

We don't say

"These colors are flattering for your type."

We say

"Let's find what is already yours."

We don't say

"Let's fix your look."

We say

"Fewer pieces, chosen well."

We don't say

"10 must-haves you need right now!"

We say

"Your palette is a starting point, not a rulebook."

We don't say

"Never wear black again."

Case

Sentence case for headlines and body. Capitals belong only to Cinzel labels and navigation.

Punctuation

No exclamation marks. No emoji in formal materials such as reports, certificates and stationery.

Vocabulary

Prefer palette, harmony, presence, intention, considered, natural. Avoid flaws, fix, hide, slimming, trendy, must-have.

The Mark

Wing II · The MarkRoom 06 — The Symbol
06

The Editorial Monogram

An open interplay between I and C. Fine curves meet a stronger serif stem, giving the symbol a confident presence and an expressive editorial character. Touch the brass points to read the details.

Use it alone

Where space is tight or the brand is already established: app icons, profile images, favicons, embossing, seals and pattern work.

Character

Expressive · Confident · Open

Never

Never outline it, fill the counters, or set the letters I and C separately in a font to imitate it.

Wing II · The MarkRoom 07 — The Wordmark
07

Precision in the Name

High-contrast classical capitals with even, generous spacing. The sweeping tail of the G echoes the open curve of the monogram's C. Pass the magnifier across the name.

Classical capitals

Thin hairlines and full stems, drawn from Roman inscription.

The sweeping G

One expressive gesture inside a calm, even name.

Master artwork

Never retype the name in a font. Always use the supplied files.

Wing II · The MarkRoom 08 — Lockups
08

Four Arrangements

The symbol and wordmark combine in fixed proportions. Pick the arrangement that suits the space; never build a new one.

Wing II · The MarkRoom 09 — Construction
09

Built on the Oval

The C is drawn from two nested ovals, which gives the curve its tapering stroke: full at the sides, a hairline at the ends. The I stem stands on the vertical centre line and divides the C into two halves.

Wing III · Color & TypeRoom 14 — Season Palettes
14

The Four Seasons

The service palette. These are the colors of the analysis itself, used on color cards, drapes and consultation reports. They never recolor the logo or the brand's own surfaces.

Rule

A season palette always sits inside a brand frame: an ivory or charcoal ground, Cinzel labels, a brass hairline. The brand holds the season, never the other way round.

Accuracy

Season colors are for presentation only. Physical drapes and fabric swatches remain the reference during a consultation.

Wing III · Color & TypeRoom 15 — Typography
15

Two Families per Script

An inscriptional face for headlines and a refined text face for reading, in Latin and in Arabic. The Arabic faces are shown here as specimens, ready for bilingual work.

Type tester

Room 15 · AnnexArabic letterforms & bilingual pairing

The Arabic Letterforms

IBM Plex Sans Arabic, every letter and numeral. Hover to inspect a glyph; drag the weight to see the family move.

Display pairing

Lifta Swash with Cinzel. Use for headlines that appear in both scripts.

Text pairing

IBM Plex Sans Arabic with Cormorant Garamond. Set the Arabic 10–15% larger to match optical size.

Alignment

Each script aligns to its own reading edge: Latin left, Arabic right.

Never

Never letterspace Arabic, never set it in capitals styling, and never fake it with a Latin face.

The Monogram, Repeated

A quiet all-over pattern for tissue paper, garment bags, gift boxes and report end pages. Keep the tone close to its ground so it reads as texture.

Wing IV · Visual LanguageRoom 18 — Graphic Elements & Icons

The Ornaments

A small set of devices that make any layout feel like Image Code, even before the logo appears. Use one or two per layout, never all at once.

Icons: 1.2 px line on a 48 px grid, round caps and joins, no fills. Hover to watch them drawn.
